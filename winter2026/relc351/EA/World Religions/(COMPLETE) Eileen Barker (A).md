What is a new religious movement?
* That is a big question that we haven't quite solved yet
* It started off as being a cult or a sect - the kind of groups that Brian Wilson wrote about
* This was very much focused on the Christo-centric religions
* Cult started out with very negative connotations, calling someone a cult told the audience more about yourself (that you don't like them) than the religion itself
* Transcendental Meditation has fought in the courts to not be defined as a religion (so that they can be taught in the schools)
* Scientology, conversely, fought to be viewed as a religion to get charity benefits

Israel, the french, Russians, and Chinese have developed laws to deal with "cults" which they define roughly as problematic or "bad groups"

I think people want to condemn by using the word cult and hide what is going on. It is a way of obfuscating. You've got to look at the particulars (to distinguish the bad groups from the benign) and you can't do that if you have a messy label.

There is no point in asking the anti-cult groups what they are like, because you already no what they will say: they will say its a cult.

In France, the catholic church has been labeled a cult

There was a lot of misinformation from the movements, from their opposition, and from the media. So she created INFORM

It seemed to me necessary to make avaliable the work of social scientists. A lot of very good work by PhD's and others was ending up in dusty libraries. INFORM was meant to help by providing accurate, reliable, and as objective as possible information about minority religions.

Important: its information NETWORK: We can't do all of the research ourselves. We have specialists, primarily academics, and we would include the religions themselves and their opponents (the anti-cult groups). Anybody who has information, we collect it from them, source it all out, and assess it and often we can't come to a definite conclusion on who is right. We have to be clear about it: we can't always get a clear picture. sometimes we right reports that can take weeks or longer. We have seminars twice a year - we invite people to give 20 minute talks (members, nonmembers, anti-cultists, academics). Big international conferences - academics and people offer papers. Closed workshops: specialists like clergy, police, welfare people, social workers, government departments, anti-cultist / cult-watching  groups from France and Belgium. 

How do you negotiate that (negotiating with police and governments) while keeping an academic rigor and objectivity?

* Comparative method
* You see headlines saying cultist commits suicide but you don't necessarily see "Anglican commits suicide" in cults
* Visibility is not the same thing as typicality
* The people we are talking to will be interested in different questions. No one tries to be objective. We are more likely to tell the inquirer about the virgin sacrifice on the altar than what this group typically has for breakfast
* We can't say whether it is a cult or a religion
* We can't say whether the religion is true or false (we have to be methodologically agnostic)
* Certainly we we began, we had a lot of strange phone calls from the movement itself or the anti-cultists asking "my son joined this cult can you tell me about it" in an attempt to trip us up. This has been a good thing for making sure that we have good, defensible information.
* Where social science can't help us, we just say, "I'm sorry, we can't help you."

**Methodology:**
In depth interviews (framework for what is going on), participant observation (living with the groups) (an idea of communication networks social structure), a lot of studies of religious movements haven't included control groups.

You can't take a new religious movement in isolation, they are dependent on the society to ge money, food, defense, and also new recruits. You have to understand the relationships with all these different sections of society.
